# M1b: primary road network, bridges, cross-machine determinism

M1b adds scope stage 4 to `citygen build`: megasite reserves, highways in the spec's pattern
with exits leaving the map, avenues that serve every district, and bridges wherever a road
crosses water, all written into the city model as `road_nodes`, `road_segments`, `bridges` and
`megasites`, with eight new checks. It also fixes the cross-machine drift found in the M1a2
review. Each city folder holds `preview_map.png`, `report.json` and `report.txt`; the specs
are unchanged and still live in `docs/progress/M1a2/` (Nyxllor in `cities/nyxllor/`).
`city_model.json` is not committed: it is regenerated byte for byte, and
`tests/data/golden_hashes.json` holds its sha256 for every sample.

## Determinism across machines

The Mac (ARM) and the cloud (x86) differed in small decimals since M1a2 (Nexus City: 4.31 vs
4.33 km of land boundary). Cause: numpy's `sin`, `cos`, `exp`, `arctan2`, `power` give
different last bits on different CPUs (x86 with AVX512 uses Intel SVML), and the district
search amplifies such bits into visible differences. Reproduced on the cloud machine by
switching numpy's AVX512 paths off (`NPY_DISABLE_CPU_FEATURES`): three of five cities changed.

- `citygen/dmath.py`: sin, cos, exp, log, atan2, hypot, power built from `+ - * /`, `sqrt`,
  `rint` and `ldexp` only (fdlibm kernels and short series after range reduction), which IEEE
  754 rounds the same everywhere; accurate to a few ULP. The generator uses these instead of
  numpy or `math` transcendentals, and 2D dot products without BLAS.
- Quantization barriers: every stage (sea, rivers, river islands, deltas, delta islands, sea
  islands, landmasses, ideal positions, district partition, separation channels, waterways,
  megasites, roads) hands on coordinates on the 0.01 m grid (`geom.qpoly`, `qline`, `qpoint`),
  so later decisions (variant score, channel budget, routing) only see grid values. Reported
  stats are rounded.
- Evidence: with SIMD on and off, all five `city_model.json` are byte-identical (before the fix
  three differed). Injecting noise of up to 1e-7 m into every geometry entering a barrier
  (`geom.JITTER_M`, a test hook) changes no byte in any of the five cities; real last-bit
  noise at city scale is about 1e-13 m.
- `tests/data/golden_hashes.json`: sha256 of each sample model, produced on the cloud machine
  (x86_64 Linux, Python 3.13, numpy 2.5.4, shapely 2.2.0 with GEOS 3.14.1); pytest asserts
  them. Running `.venv/bin/python -m pytest tests/test_roads.py -k golden` on the M4 Mac
  confirms them there.

## How the roads are built (`citygen/roads.py`)

1. **Raster.** 25 m cells: land, canal-class water (separation channels, canals, creeks,
   inlets, marinas), river-class water (rivers, delta branches) or sea-class water (sea, sea
   channels, harbor slips as port approaches). Megasite reserves, flight cones and open land
   (marsh, park) are closed; cells next to quays cost more. Water costs per meter and once per
   entry (sea far more than river, river more than canal), so routes cross at narrow points
   and major bridges stay few; joining or crossing an existing bridge out on the water is
   priced out (no junction on a bridge).
2. **Routing.** A* over 16 neighbors; later routes reuse earlier roads and add only their new
   parts, joined at T-junctions. Named bridges may only use the land of their two ends.
3. **Crossings.** Each water run becomes the shortest straight crossing between the banks
   near it (it may pass over a land tip of up to 40 m), with straight approaches long enough
   for the turn that follows at the class radius.
4. **Fitting.** Douglas-Peucker, then a circular arc (fillet) at every corner; each corner first
   gets the tangent length its minimum radius needs. A corner that still cannot hold the
   minimum is removed when the shortcut stays clear of closed areas and water.
5. **Highways.** ring: sector waypoints at about 0.6 of the mainland radius, one closed loop;
   spine: exit, city center, far end (stopping short of a megasite gate); ring_plus_spine:
   both (the spine reuses the ring where they meet); grid: lines about 1.1 km apart on the
   principal axis; radial: spokes from the center to every exit plus spokes to the far land
   until four; coastal: 180 m inland of the main coast; none: exits by avenue. Exits not
   carried by a named bridge sit where the highway comes closest to that map edge (a straight
   stub). Named highway routes follow their stops.
6. **Avenues.** In order: axis boulevards (rotated grids), ring roads (transit hubs, around
   central parks), waterfront avenues 50 m from major water, avenues on the land boundaries
   between districts, and chords at the district grid angle until no point is farther than
   about half the archetype spacing (350 m corporate core to 700 m spaceport) from a road.
   Stretches running along an existing road are dropped.
7. **Bridges and connectivity.** Named bridges (an existing crossing between the same two
   places over the asked water is named instead of building a second), then connectors until
   every landmass with a district is reached, then river and canal crossings by
   `water.bridges.density`; footbridges last, away from road bridges.
8. **Assembly.** Noding, degree-2 merges (sharp joints refitted), dangles and noding loops
   removed, short segments collapsed, a final refit of any segment under the minimum radius,
   optional roads cut at curves that cannot be fixed, roads that cross out on the water grade
   separated (no node; both record it), dead ends near the map edge extended to it (they run
   off the map; node kind `edge`, not an exit).
9. **Road variants.** If a check fails, the roads are rebuilt with up to three other
   variants (ring radius, ring sector rotation, highway shore cost) and the best kept. All five
   samples pass on the first.

Model: `road_segment` has `road_class` (type `road.<class>`), `lanes`, `width_m`, a cross
section (scope 5.1: highway 2 x 3 lanes 30 m; avenue 2 x 2 lanes 28 m; axis boulevard 2 x 3
lanes 38 m), `level` 0, a placeholder `z_profile` (ground, 6% ramps, deck over water),
`bridge`, `bridge_ids`, `clearance_m` and `district_ids` (districts it serves); `attrs` hold the
role (ring, spine, exit, waterfront, boundary, chord, ...), `elevated` for highways and
`grade_separated_with`. `bridge` has `water_class`, `clearance_m`, `span_m` and
`ramp_length_m` $= \frac{\Delta h}{g}$ with $\Delta h$ = clearance + 1.5 m deck and $g = 0.06$
(a sea bridge: $\frac{26.5}{0.06} \approx 442$ m per side). `megasite` has the reserve polygon
and, for spaceports, `flight_cone`.

## Checks added (scope section 9, road parts)

| Check | Rule |
|---|---|
| road_graph | one component; every landmass with a district reached; every exit built |
| districts_served | every district has at least 50 m of avenue within 25 m |
| named_bridges | every `water.bridges.named` entry built |
| bridge_clearance | clearance matches the water crossed (sea 25 m+, river 10 to 15 m, canal 3 to 4 m), ramps from the 6% rule, every road over water is a bridge, no junction on a bridge |
| road_avoidance | no road through megasite reserves, flight cones, marsh or park (declared causeways aside) |
| curve_radius | highway 150 m, avenue 40 m, measured on 20 m and 10 m chords, outside junction areas (25 m from an avenue junction, 60 m from a highway junction, which becomes an interchange in M7) |
| segment_length | highway 60 m, avenue 40 m (a stretch cut by the map border is not counted) |
| road_duplicates | no two roads within 25 m of each other for over 150 m (60 m around shared points aside) |

`placement` now also checks `separated_by: highway` and `ring_road` (200 m of it within 60 m of
the district boundary).

## Still weak

- The road layer is sensitive to small geometry changes: on 12 other seeds of the small test
  city, 10 pass every check and 2 miss one highway curve (75 to 120 m against 150 m); the road
  variants did not rescue those. The samples pass on the first variant.
- Highways meet at grade (merges and crossings) and sometimes curve hard within 60 m of such a
  junction; the radius check leaves those areas to M7 interchanges.
- Several spaceport and freight terminal reserves shrink to fit their districts (noted in the
  reports); flight cones are drawn away from the city center, which suits coastal sites.
- Roads near the map edge run off the map (avenues only); real exits are only the spec's.
- Ring highways go where the land allows: Nyxllor's runs over Scholar Isle, Nexus's crosses the
  Sentinel Canal twice.
- Dead-end avenues remain at megasite gates and on narrow land (Hook Spaceport, Launch Key).
