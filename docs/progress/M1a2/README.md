# M1a2: visual-quality pass on water, districts and waterways

M1a was correct but read as geometric: convex pie-slice districts, ruler-straight 30 m
separation canals on almost every boundary, plain rivers, stub deltas, rectangular harbor
basins and tangled canals. M1a2 reworks stages 1 to 3 so the five sample cities read as organic
coastal cities, before M1b lays highways and bridges along these boundaries and channels.

Each city folder holds `before_after.png` (the M1a preview next to the new one),
`preview_map.png`, `report.json` and `report.txt`; the four M0 cities also hold the spec they
were built from. Vesper Bay is built from `cities/vesper_bay/city_spec.json`. `city_model.json` is
not committed: it is regenerated byte for byte from the spec (checked across two processes with
different hash seeds for all five cities).

All ten checks pass for all five cities. Build times on the cloud container: 1.5 s to 35 s.

Measures (all in `report.json` stats):
- **Boundary between districts on water** (`boundary_on_water`): every district outline is
  sampled every 10 m; a sample is a land boundary when another district is within 2 m, a water
  boundary when the nearest other district is within 250 m across water. The share is
  $\frac{L_\text{water}}{L_\text{water} + L_\text{land}}$. M1a measured 100% in every city (every
  land boundary had been cut). `water_required` cities stay at 100% by design.
- **Perimeter facing water** (`district_boundary_on_water`): the M1a measure, district outline
  (map edges aside) within 2 m of water, open coast included.
- **Boundary curvature**: mean of $\frac{\text{arc}}{\text{chord}}$ over 400 m windows of
  district-to-district boundary; a straight power-diagram edge gives $1.0$. Land boundaries
  alone (`land_boundary_curvature`): 1.03 (Vesper Bay), 1.02 (Nexus), 1.03 (Greyhook).
- Separation channel sinuosity (length / chord): M1a 1.00, M1a2 1.02 to 1.05; widths M1a
  30 m, M1a2 25 to 60 m. Rivers (length / chord): M1a 1.00, M1a2 1.04 to 1.10.
- Canal problems (crossings below 30 degrees, near duplicates; the new `canal_quality` check):
  M1a models had 2 (Vesper Bay), 3 (Saltreach), 1 (Delta Megacity); M1a2 has none.

Waterway km fell because the canal networks are now sparse trees with spacing rules (and
harbor slips replace large rectangles); quays follow.

## What changed

1. **Organic district boundaries** (`districts.py`). Districts are power cells of a
   domain-warped position $p + V(p)$ ($V$: smooth 2D fractal noise, amplitude about 0.4 of a
   district radius): boundaries are the preimages of straight power-diagram edges, so they are
   curved and irregular, some districts are concave, and it is still an exact partition. Weights
   are iterated on a raster, boundaries are iso-lines of the power differences (contourpy, so
   smooth, not stepped), shared edges are simplified as a coverage, and detached bits are
   measured on the final polygons and corrected for up to three rounds, so shares stay within
   tolerance. Districts are still one piece each.
2. **Separation channels and the `water_preferred` budget.** Channels follow the (now curved)
   boundary with a sine-generated meander, 25 m at an inland end widening to up to 80 m where
   they open onto the sea (wider for longer channels). New spec field
   `water.boundary_water_share` (default 0.6): chains of one to three boundaries that run from
   water to water are cut, those already near water first, until the share of
   district-to-district boundary on water reaches the target. A boundary that only reaches the
   map edge is never a reason to cut; water-to-edge chains are a fallback when the
   water-to-water ones cannot reach the budget, edge-to-edge never. `water_required` keeps full
   separation. Achieved share in the report, the preview inset and the table above.
3. **Rivers** (`hydro.py`, `geom.course`). Sine-generated meander curves (Langbein and Leopold)
   with varying amplitude and wavelength, about 11 widths between bends, widening downstream
   with smooth banks. A delta river now crosses most of the city (the coast grows a lobe for
   the delta). Tributaries join a little downstream of their entry at 45 degrees to the parent's
   flow instead of aiming at the map center; `to: {"edge": "<parent>", "at": t}` pins the
   junction (new).
4. **Deltas.** Distributaries split well inland (the split is pushed upstream until each branch
   runs seaward, and mid-river islands move upstream to make room), curve and fan out to
   mouths spread along the coast; the main channel narrows below the split. For bay, crescent
   and straight coasts the coast grows a lobe at the mouth, so Vesper Bay's river no longer ends
   at the map center. A delta island is sized by scaling the whole fan by bisection. No orphan
   wedges: land without a district becomes named open land (`open_spaces`, kind `marsh` beside
   delta channels, else `park`), reported in the stats and checked by the new `land_use`
   check.
5. **Inner waterways** (`waterways.py`). Harbor basins are a row of parallel rectangular slips
   (70 to 95 m wide, piers 110 to 150 m) cut square or at a common angle into the longest
   straight stretch of deep-water frontage; marinas are rounded basins behind the shore with a
   short entrance. Organic canals and creeks are a sparse tree: a canal stops before it comes
   within 70 m of another canal or runs along the district's shore, and may close one of at
   most two loops only when it meets the network at 50 degrees or more. Canal grids follow the
   district grid angle and are trimmed so every canal ends at water or at a cross canal (the
   map border is not a quay).
6. **Placement misses.**
   - Nexus City's Kurosawa River: a generator problem. Tributaries aimed at the map center, and
     variants also shifted unpinned tributary entries, so it crossed the city diagonally. With
     the new tributary routing it runs down the east side and joins the Sentinel Canal
     downstream, as in the concept. The spec now also pins its entry
     (`from: {"edge": "north_east", "at": 0.42}`): the land east of it is Midtown's bank, so
     the entry point sets Midtown's share.
   - Saltreach's Lumen Key: both. The generator placed islands nearest an aim point with no
     bearing constraint; it now tries every gap inside a 35 degree sector toward the asked
     position first, retries with missed islands first, and notes any island that still has no
     room. Here there is no room: with Crown Island in the middle of the sea, the west side
     holds Westreef (the western island the prompt asks for) or a southwest key, not both. The
     prompt never places the research island, so the spec now says `north_west`.
7. **Preview.** Shallow-water bands along the shore, thinner district borders, open land drawn
   and named, labels placed so that none overlap (each tries several spots inside its feature
   and smaller sizes), and an inset bar with the boundary-on-water share against its target.

## Still weak

- Delta Megacity builds 2 of its 3 delta branches: the 0.55 km² Central Island in mid-river and
  the 0.5 km² Delta Island leave little river below the island, so the fan is shallow and the
  third mouth finds no room on the coast.
- The district layout is more sensitive to the hydrology variant than in M1a: Vesper Bay passes on
  variant 18 of 20, Nexus on 11, Delta Megacity on 12 (the search now tries up to 20 variants).
  Most misses are an `Old Town` that loses the river bank or a share that drifts after the
  channels are cut.
- Vesper Bay's `boundary_on_water` is 53% against a 60% target: the next channel would overshoot
  further than it would help. Vesper Bay notes that Launch Key sits north of west from the middle
  of the bay (the bay is narrow there).
- Boundaries meet the shore at whatever angle the warp gives; there is no explicit preference
  for boundaries that hug water, only for channels on boundaries already near water.
- Canal networks are clean but sparse; Nexus and Vesper Bay old towns have three to four canals.
