# AG18 brand book

Seven made-up makers. A brand is a design language in geometry and texture: how a body is
cut, what its lights, wheels, grilles and trims are shaped like, how its panels are finished
and lettered. Color is never part of it: every car will be deployed in many colors, so each
car picks a show color of its own for its sheet and nothing about a brand depends on it.

A car of a brand should be recognisable as its sibling's relative with the paint stripped
off, and should never be mistaken for another brand's.

## Halcyon (performance)

Creased wedges, low and wide, mid-engined proportions even on its larger cars.

- A darker structural spine from the nose over the roof to the tail, the flanks in body
  color either side of it.
- Octagonal wheel arches with bolted trims.
- A thin light strip under the nose lip that turns down each corner; a light bar round the
  whole perimeter of the tail.
- Honeycomb mesh in every opening; louvres over the engine cover; a scoop ahead of the rear
  wheel.
- Twin-spoke turbine wheels with a light ring on the rim.
- Lettering: wide-spaced capitals on the tail beam, model name low on the door.

## Kestrel Aerodyne (hover, sharp)

No wheels. Hulls like folded blades: flat facets, knife edges, a pointed prow.

- Lift comes from emitter pods at the corners (rings or slotted plates on the underside,
  glowing), carried on short pylons or faired into the hull; landing skids fold beside them.
- A keel line along the underside with a light blade; ride height 0.3 to 0.45 m.
- Twin canted tail fins; thruster slots across the stern rather than exhausts.
- Glass set flush in facets, never bubbles.
- Panel texture: fine parallel ribbing on the lower hull and the emitter housings.
- Lettering: registration codes like aircraft (KH-2, a tail number), stencil style.

| Meridian | hover sedan | 4 | long cabin, four pods, the calm one |
| Atlas | hover hauler | 2 + load bed | a flat bed behind the cab, larger pods, tie-down rails |

## Orrin (smooth, organic)

Pebbles and drops. One continuous surface with almost no creases, as if poured.

- A glass canopy that flows from the nose to the tail as one piece; the body wraps up
  around it.
- No visible grille: air goes in through slim crescent slots; shut lines follow the curves.
- Light ribbons that run as unbroken lines round the nose and tail, following the body's
  flow.
- Flying buttresses and through-body channels on the faster cars (a gap you can see
  through between the cabin and the rear fenders).
- Wheels as smooth discs with a glowing ring or a turbine of many fine vanes, set flush.
- Panel texture: satin, uninterrupted; fine perforated patterns where it must breathe.
- Lettering: small, lower case, one word.

|---|---|---|---|

| Pebble | city pod | 2 | under 3 m, nearly symmetrical front to back |
| Aria | hypercar | 2 | the canopy as a teardrop, buttresses, the widest hips |

## Brakk Industrial (heavy, utility)

Monoliths. Flat plates with big chamfers, built to be repaired with a wrench.

- Wheels exposed or half exposed: no flush wells, separate fender plates or none, tall
  tires with deep tread, visible suspension arms.
- Visible fasteners in rows, ribbed and corrugated panels, tread plate, rub strips.
- Vertical light bars at the corners and a full-width bar above the windshield.
- Tow hooks, winch housings, rack rails, tie-down points, roll bars: real hardware.
- High ground clearance, short overhangs, upright glass in thick frames.
- Lettering: big stencilled capitals and unit numbers, hazard chevrons.

| Bastion | armored utility vehicle | 4 | slab sides, small glass, roof rack and light bar |
| Mule | cargo van | 2 + cargo | six wheels, a ribbed box body, side and rear doors |

## Aurel (luxury, formal)

Long, calm and upright. Razor shoulder lines on big quiet surfaces.

- A long hood, a formal roofline, a slab nose with a tall "grille" made of vertical slots
  of light rather than an opening.
- Very few lines: one shoulder crease nose to tail, one sill line; no vents, no wings.
- Large wheels covered by flat or shallow-dished discs with a fine radial pattern.
- Bright trim strips (polished metal) framing the glass and running along the sill.
- Lights as slim vertical blades at the corners, front and rear.
- Panel texture: mirror smooth; a fine fluted pattern on the lower sill and the grille
  surround.
- Lettering: a small crest and a serif-like wordmark, sparing.

| Regent | limousine | 4 | over 5.6 m, a formal rear cabin |
| Zenith | hover grand tourer | 4 | Aurel's calm on hidden emitters: no wheels, no arches |

## Vex (skeletal, open)

Race machinery with the skin left off. The car is pods joined by struts.

- Wheels outside the body, on visible wishbones, with separate cycle fenders or none;
  hubless rim-driven wheels on some.
- Gaps through the body: the nose, the cabin pod and the side pods are separate volumes
  with air between them; wings and floors carry them.
- A central spine or keel that everything hangs from; the cockpit open or under a slim
  canopy.
- Exposed structure: trusses, pushrods, radiators, cable runs.
- Lights as small clusters on stalks and thin strips along wing edges.
- Panel texture: bare weave and raw machined metal, drilled lightening holes.
- Lettering: race numbers and small sponsor-like blocks, all made up.

## Tanager (everyday, urban)

The cars most people in the city actually drive. Friendly boxes with big glass.

- Tall cabins, short noses, the wheels at the corners; sliding doors on the larger ones.
- Rounded-off rectangles everywhere: windows, lamps, the body's own corners.
- A single horizontal light bar across the nose and one across the tail, with a small
  display panel beside it.
- Plastic lower cladding with a fine molded grain, bump strips, steel wheels with simple
  covers.
- Roof gear by job: a taxi sign, a cargo rack, a sensor mast.
- Lettering: fleet numbers, service markings, a round badge.
